## Readout

This sample shows that the harvester can find companies investing in AWS/Azure skills. It does **not** demonstrate that those companies want to outsource work. A job opening can mean they intend to build in-house, and a services company should treat it as a timely reason to research the account—not as proof of purchase intent.

Keep separate signal labels:

- **Internal hiring signal:** a company is recruiting cloud expertise.
- **Project signal:** posting mentions migration, platform buildout, security, reliability, cost, or modernization.
- **Buying signal:** a public RFP, partner request, explicit consultant/contractor need, or direct confirmation from a buyer.

These three examples are internal hiring signals with project details. None qualifies as a confirmed buying signal. Do not tell a prospect “we know you need an MSP” based on these ads.

## Recommended first validation

Ask 5–10 manufacturing-focused MSP owners or sales leaders to review this sample. The goal is to learn whether they would investigate these accounts, what extra information would make the feed actionable, and which signal types they would pay for. Do not sell the sample as validated intent data yet.

**LinkedIn connection note**

> Hi [Name] — I’m testing a small feed that tracks public AWS/Azure hiring signals at manufacturers. I have three recent examples and would value a quick reality check from someone who sells cloud services. Open to a short conversation?

**Follow-up after they agree**

> Thanks. Here are three source-linked examples. They show internal hiring and project areas, not confirmed outsourcing needs. Which, if any, would your team pursue? What would you need to know before calling it useful?

Use manual, individualized outreach. Do not automate LinkedIn connection requests or messages. If switching to cold commercial email, use accurate sender/subject details, identify the commercial nature, include a valid postal address and opt-out method, and honor opt-outs.
